# Revontulet: what each measure is, and the study idea behind it

Revontulet is Lanvi's 90-second game: pick a fault line in the ice (easy or hard), trace a half-circle, pull the spark back like a slingshot and let go, tap the embers on a hard shot, then stroke the sleeping fox. The app sends it as a `focus` record with one extra object, `revontulet` (shape and limits: `docs/api/README.md` and `openapi.json`, `RevontuletIn`).

The server stores these numbers and shows them to the clinician. **It does not yet use them** to judge a day, pick suggestions, count XP or build the person's own normal. Nothing here is validated in Lanvi; the studies below are where each idea comes from, with the numbers as the research summary for this game gives them (a secondary summary, not checked against the original papers).

| Measure | What it is | Study idea behind it |
|---|---|---|
| `rounds[].effort` | Easy (thin low crack, one shot) or hard (thick vein, two embers, a bigger reward). | The Effort-Expenditure for Rewards Task (EEfRT, Treadway et al.): on each trial a person picks a low-effort "easy task" for a small fixed reward or a high-effort "hard task" for a higher, variable reward. Anhedonia predicts fewer hard-task choices. The summary gives the share of hard-task choices on medium-probability trials correlating with high- and low-probability trials at r = 0.31 (p < 0.05). Revontulet has 3 choices per game and no probability tiers, so it is a far smaller, gamified cousin of the task. |
| `rounds[].choice_ms`, `trace_start_ms` | Fault lines shown to the tap that picked one; the half-circle appearing to the first touch on it. | No study number. Plain decision and start times, kept so the choice and the trace can be read alongside how long the person took to begin. |
| `rounds[].trace_ms`, `trace_length_pt`, `trace_mean_speed_pt_s`, `trace_peak_speed_pt_s`, `trace_lifts` | How the half-circle was traced: time, path length, mean and peak finger speed, lifts. The half-circle turns a quarter turn each round. | Drawing kinematics: a tablet study (N = 40: 20 youths with a DSM-5 depressive disorder, mean age 14.65, and 20 healthy controls) used tasks including tracing four single-stroke semicircles rotated clockwise in 90° steps, and reached 82.5% subject-level classification accuracy from 3 velocity-related features. The summary adds that velocity scaling (peak velocity scaled to stroke length) gives stronger and more consistent effects than raw velocity. Different device, age group and task length from Lanvi. |
| `rounds[].pull_pt`, `pull_hold_ms` | How far the spark was pulled back, and how long it was held before the release. | No study number. Kept as a second, simple motor measure next to the trace. |
| `rounds[].embers_shown`, `embers_hit`, `bonus` | Embers that flashed on a hard shot, how many were tapped in time, and whether the surprise extra reward came. | Zero-punishment design and positive reward prediction errors: missing never costs progress; successes give larger-than-expected rewards. A design rule from the summary, not a measure with a study number. The ember reaction times already go into `reaction_ms`. |
| `strokes[].ms`, `length_pt`, `mean_speed_pt_s` | The slow strokes over the sleeping fox. | In-game swipe speed: the summary reports average swipe speed correlating inversely with PHQ-8 (ρ = −0.405, p < 0.001) in a mobile game study. The strokes are also the C-tactile haptic moment of the game; that part is about the feel, not a measure. |
| `touch_radius_mean_pt`, `touch_radius_var` | Mean and variance of the touch radius (`UITouch.majorRadius`). | Swipe pressure variance correlating inversely with PHQ-8 (ρ = −0.326, p = 0.004) in the same kind of study. **An iPhone does not report pressure here**: touch radius is only a stand-in, and the number may not behave like pressure. |
| `broke_through_s` | Seconds from the start to the wall bursting (null if the fox's own spark had to finish it). | No study number. Marks when the celebration (and the app's pleasant moment for the face) happened. |
| `fox_finished` | True when time ran out and the fox's own spark broke the wall, so `broke_through_s` was not the person's doing. | No study number. Tells apart games where the person broke the wall from games where the fox finished it. |
| `pt_per_cm` | Screen points per centimetre on that phone. | Lets speeds in points per second be turned into cm per second, so phones of different sizes can be compared. |
