# Junction 2026 challenges: the 8 simple ones

Junction has 19 challenges. These are the 8 that need no robots, no special hardware and no deep AI research. Each one is mostly an app or a web tool with a good idea behind it, which is where we are strongest.

Source: the Junction platform, challenges page (hackjunction.app/participate/45/challenges, needs a login). Summaries are ours, read the full text there before we commit.

Nothing is chosen yet. Read these, say which 2 or 3 you like, then we pick one together and write it into the README.

## 1. Exa: The Internet Butler

**In one line:** an AI assistant that does boring life research for people and gets the task almost done.

**What they want:** people waste hours digging for answers that exist online but are buried. Things like "is my rent increase legal?", "which benefits can my family get?", "my train got cancelled, what now?". Exa is a search engine made for AI, it finds and reads web pages and hands back clean data. Build an agent that uses Exa to find the answer, work out what needs to happen, and do as much of it as it can. The user only gives the final OK.

**How they judge:** it must find things the AI could not know from its training alone. It must be useful to one specific group of real people. It must show its sources. The judges will type their own questions into the live demo, so it has to really work.

**Prize:** 1st $2,500 cash + $5,000 Exa credits. 2nd $1,500 + $3,000 credits. 3rd $500 + $1,000 credits.

## 2. S-Pankki (S-Bank): Personal Economic Trainer

**In one line:** an AI money coach you can chat with about your own spending.

**What they want:** S-Bank is the 4th biggest bank in Finland, 3.5 million customers. They give us a big fake dataset of bank transactions that looks like their real data. Build a chat where people ask things like "how much did I spend on taxis last month?", "what kind of spender am I?" or "what is safe to spend this week?". No Finnish bank has this yet.

**What they like:** teaching people where money goes, using 3 baskets. Fixed and regular (rent). Fixed but surprise (broken washing machine). Small stuff that slips away (coffees). Let people explore their own data freely, not just fixed charts. Comparing with public Finnish statistics is a plus. Build it as a mobile app or a web app that fits inside a phone app.

**Prize:** Airbnb gift cards. 1st €2,000, 2nd €1,000, 3rd €500.

## 3. Context.dev: The Web Is Your Real-Time Database

**In one line:** an app that watches the internet live and acts on what it sees.

**What they want:** most software uses old data. Context.dev turns any website, company, person or news story into clean data with one call. Build something that turns live web data into a decision, an action or an experience that was not possible before. Their examples: compare GPU rental prices across providers, rank startup founders by how active they are on X, watch for new security holes, or make a sign-up screen that changes based on the customer's company.

**Prize:** 1st €2,000 + swag. 2nd €500 + swag. 3rd swag.

## 4. Cala: Make Unicorns Real

**In one line:** a tool for startup founders or investors, built on Cala's data about companies, funding and investors.

**What they want:** founders and investors decide big things from messy browser tabs and gut feeling. Cala is a clean, sourced database of companies, people, funding rounds and investors, and how they connect. Build one product that solves one real need. For example, help a first-time founder find the right investors, or help a small fund pick which industry to bet on. Slush (the big startup event) starts right after Junction, so they want something people could use the next day.

**How they judge:** 25% real need, 25% it works end to end, 25% fresh idea, 25% Cala is the core of it (not an add-on). One thing done really well beats ten things done halfway.

**Prize:** each winner gets about €5,000 of free hackathon trips (one hackathon a month for 3 months, travel and hotel paid) + €1,000 Cala credits. Runner-up gets €500 credits.

## 5. City of Espoo: Rethinking Recruitment

**In one line:** help companies and job seekers really find each other at Espoo's hiring events.

**What they want:** now everyone writes CVs and job ads with AI, so it is hard to tell who fits. Espoo runs recruitment and matchmaking events and wants them to work better. The platform has only this one question, no more detail. We would need to ask at their stand.

**Prize:** 1st €1,500, 2nd €1,000, 3rd €500.

## 6. AstraZeneca: Equal Care Across Finland

**In one line:** make sure cancer patients everywhere in Finland get diagnosed and treated equally fast.

**What they want:** build an AI tool that does 3 things. Finds where some regions or groups get worse or slower cancer care. Shows where in the process it goes wrong (spotting it early, being sent from the local doctor to the hospital, diagnosis, or starting treatment). Recommends what to change. The recommendation part matters most. It could be an AI analyst, a dashboard with advice, an early warning for delays, or a "what if we changed this" simulation.

**Must have:** public data or info from their mentors, a source for every claim, at least one measure from Goal 3 of Finland's national cancer plan, and a working demo (slides alone are not enough). They explain the challenge at their stand on Friday at 20:00.

**Prize:** 1st €1,000, 2nd €750, 3rd €250.

## 7. Aalto University: Beyond the Clinic

**In one line:** an app or product that helps people stay well at home after depression treatment.

**What they want:** some people with severe depression get brain stimulation treatment (TMS) in a clinic, with visits almost every day. It often works, but the depression can come back. At home there are no doctors or brain scans. They want a home "maintenance" product using anything: a phone, music, games, light, nature, AI. Best if it works as a loop: notice how the person is doing, do something that helps, learn what works for that person.

**Prize:** not announced yet.

## 8. Meta: AI Glasses for Everyone, Everyday

**In one line:** a useful everyday app for Meta's smart glasses that uses what you are looking at.

**What they want:** show how seeing from the wearer's eyes makes an everyday task easier, hands-free and in the moment. It should help people of all abilities (for example people with poor sight). It can use the glasses' display or be audio only. It can be a web app or a phone app using Meta's toolkit. They want a working prototype plus a short deck, a clear problem and a clear audience.

**Note:** we need the glasses. Ask Meta's stand if they lend them.

**Prize:** each member of the winning team gets Ray-Ban Meta glasses.
