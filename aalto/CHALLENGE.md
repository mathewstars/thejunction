# Aalto University: Beyond the Clinic

Our challenge for Junction 2026. This is our summary of the brief on the Junction platform (hackjunction.app, Challenges, "Beyond the Clinic", needs a login). Read the original before you build against a detail.

## The task

Build the product that helps people stay well at home after depression treatment, using anything from natural stimuli to a phone, music, games, light or AI.

## Background

Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) is used in hospitals and clinics to treat people with severe depression. It usually needs several brief daily visits. Even when the treatment works, there is a risk that the depression returns, so Aalto wants a "maintenance" treatment at home.

In the hospital there are blood samples, brain scans (fMRI, MEG, EEG) and a psychiatrist. At home there are none of these, apart from an occasional remote psychotherapy meeting, which is hard to get or expensive. The brief says no good solution for maintenance at home exists yet.

The treatment at home could be "closed-loop": individualised by how the patient reacts. In their words, the product can work as a loop: sense how someone is doing, respond with something that helps, and learn what works best for that person.

## Who we can design for

- People after TMS treatment, who want to keep feeling well once the daily visits end.
- Anyone at risk of depression, who could benefit from early, everyday support.
- Clinicians, who want to follow how their patients are doing between visits without more workload.

## Ideas from the brief

No special hardware is needed. A laptop and a phone are enough.

**What could help people stay well**

- Sensory stimulation (sound, sight, touch, smell, taste), for example music or rhythmic lights or colours.
- Social interaction, meditation, yoga and physical exercise.
- Regular visits to forests or the sea, or the same in VR when going out is hard.
- Computer games, such as the one from Soihtu (soihtudtx.com).

**How to sense how someone is doing at home**

- The phone: sounds, voice, movement (acceleration), facial expressions, sleep and daily routines.
- Wearables such as smart rings: heart signals (HRV), sleep, activity and changes in the autonomic nervous system, which the brief calls a very important indicator of mental state.

**Product directions they would love to see**

- Everyday companion: a phone or AI check-in that notices changes over time and suggests the right thing at the right moment.
- Sensory experience: music, light or VR that adapts to how the person is doing that day.
- Game: something fun to play daily that also supports mood and wellbeing.
- Smart home: lights and sound that gently change through the day.
- Social: a way for friends or peers to stay close and reach out when it matters.
- Clinician view: a simple way for professionals to see who might need a call.

For inspiration they name Meru Health (meruhealth.com).

## Judging

| Criterion | Weight | Their question |
|---|---|---|
| Usefulness | 25% | Could it really help people stay well at home? |
| Ease of use | 25% | Would someone recovering from depression actually use it every day? |
| Smart sensing | 25% | Does it make clever use of what can be measured at home, and learn what works for each person? |
| Creativity | 25% | How original and delightful is the idea? |

## What they provide

- Pointers to published studies and openly available datasets. Nothing is handed over directly; ask the mentors for the list.
- Mentors: researchers from the brain-stimulation group at the Department of Neuroscience and Biomedical Engineering, plus Aalto Innovation.
- Two demos, subject to confirmation with the lab: a live TMS demo and a portable EEG at the booth.

No dataset, API or hardware comes with the challenge.

## What already exists (from their own references, checked on 13 Nov)

- **Soihtu:** a video game as a digital therapeutic for adult depression, based on executive-function training, trialled at Aalto. Their site describes self-adapting mechanics. It says nothing about using play data to sense how the player is doing.
- **Meru Health:** a 12-week program with a licensed therapist, an app and a small device for heart-rate-variability biofeedback.
- **AR4U (Aalto):** a voice AI companion that calls older adults for daily check-ins. Named in Aalto's profile text on the platform.
